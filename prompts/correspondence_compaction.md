# Correspondence Compaction

Two storytellers keep a private correspondence behind the screen; this call maintains its digest.

The digest is the pair's memory of their own conspiracy: every plan, seed, and promise from letters too old to carry verbatim, held compactly enough to ride into every future turn.

The input is the current digest, the exchanges now aging out of the verbatim window, and the most recent exchanges for bearings on where the story stands.

The task is to fold the aging exchanges into a new digest that supersedes the old one entirely.

Every plan extracted from the aging letters receives a judgment of where it stands now: **live** — still viable, worth pursuing; **stale** — overtaken by events but salvageable; or **retired** — expired, moot, impossible, or superseded, with a word on why.

Live plans keep enough specificity to act on — who, what, and the condition that fires them; a plan compressed past usefulness is a plan lost.

Retired plans survive as a single line each in a closed-threads section, so the pair never re-seeds what they already abandoned.

The digest records authorial intent, never world canon: it asserts nothing about what has happened in the story, only what the storytellers meant and still mean to do.

Nothing is invented: a plan absent from the letters and the old digest does not exist.

The output is the complete new digest and nothing else, compact enough to be read every turn without regret.

The complete digest must fit within {{MAX_DIGEST_TOKENS}} tokens.
